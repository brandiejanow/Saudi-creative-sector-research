Research design / Ethics / Source integrity

Methods

A public description of how this historical record is selected, examined and prepared for citation.

Independent archive review   |   Documentary, qualitative and comparative methods

01 / Research design

Retrospective study with continuing observation.

The project uses a longitudinal archive of dated documents and discussions to examine questions about Saudi Arabia's design industry and creative sector across 2019 to 2026. It combines historical documentation, qualitative interpretation, public statistical context and future research questions.

Records were originally produced for different purposes and under different institutional relationships. Their inclusion in a source inventory does not imply a uniform research protocol, random sample, consent for publication, or common research question. The archive is therefore being screened before any claim is included in a public paper.

02 / Units of analysis

The study is not a census of creative professionals.

A source can be a recorded meeting, document, interview, field note, photograph, published report, or official statistical release. A source is not the same as one unique participant, one firm, or one finding.

A duplicate recording or transcript export is not counted as a second research event. A planning discussion is marked as planning evidence. An applicant's account is treated as one account, not as proof of national prevalence.

03 / Review procedure

A repeatable process for historical material.

1. Establish source identity. Record a stable identifier, original date, author or responsible institution, file type, and context at collection.

2. Preserve the original. Working notes, transcripts and excerpts are derived from a retained source; original materials remain in private storage.

3. Classify the evidence. Distinguish direct observation, interview account, proposal, organizational decision, public measurement and later analysis.

4. Code within the source's scope. Record recurring subjects and contrasting accounts without assuming that a theme was equally prevalent across the entire country.

5. Compare across years. Check whether similar terms refer to comparable conditions, places, groups, and institutions.

6. Look for counterevidence. Record contrary examples, different participant experiences, and the limits of the author's position.

7. Check release rights. Verify permission, attribution, anonymity, confidentiality and organizational ownership before publishing a quotation or document.

8. Link the claim. A published statement identifies the record supporting it, how that record was read, and what the reader must not infer from it.

04 / Researcher position

Proximity offers access but does not remove bias.

The author lived and worked in Saudi Arabia for many years and has been involved in creative-sector activity, professional relationships, and program development. This provides firsthand context while also creating reasons for careful disclosure of personal interests, selective access, prior assumptions, and disagreement with institutions or participants.

The publication will distinguish the author's experience from testimony, independent documentation, government reports, and interpretation. Assertions concerning individuals, companies, employment conditions or institutional motives require source checking and fair treatment.

05 / Data and quotations

Direct wording has a higher release threshold.

No identifiable participant transcript, unpublished client file, original audio, private contact data, or confidential organizational record is included in this public repository. Quoted words from an archival participant require verification of the recording and permission or another defensible basis for publication.

Brief quotations from the author's 2025 research drafts are labelled as statements in a proposal, not as verbatim participant accounts. The drafts remain private while archival rights and source context are reviewed.

Official data is attributed to its publisher. A survey proportion is not presented as a result of the author's independent research, and unmatched denominators are not combined to create a trend.

06 / Limits of the current edition

Open questions stay open.

This public edition is not yet a completed journal article, nationally representative dataset, or final longitudinal evaluation. It is the research framework, an initial source-based paper dossier, and a public reference register.

The historical archive includes items that are not research at all. Private totals must therefore be audited for relevance before reporting a study count. Later publication will make coding decisions, exclusions, source date disagreements and missing evidence visible to readers.

The Rukun Saudi Creative Index is a distinct organizational study. Its internal records are not incorporated into this independent publication.
