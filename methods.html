Research design / Ethics / Source integrity

Methods

A public description of how this historical record is selected, examined and prepared for citation.

Independent archive review   |   Documentary, qualitative and comparative methods

1 / Research design

Study design

The study analyzes original qualitative material collected since 2019, historical documents, and published statistical information to examine professional conditions in Saudi Arabia's design industry and creative sector.

The sources originated in different professional settings. Coding procedures consider the date, context, and scope of each account.

2 / Units of analysis

Units of analysis

The unit of analysis varies by source: an observation, recorded account, document, or published indicator. Duplicate archival copies are identified before coding.

A participant account represents that perspective within its original context and is not a statistically representative national measure.

3 / Review procedure

Source selection and analytical procedure

1. Establish source identity. Record a stable identifier, original date, author or responsible institution, file type, and context at collection.

2. Preserve the original. Analytical notes and anonymized extracts are derived from retained sources; originals stay in private storage.

3. Classify the evidence. Distinguish direct observation, interview account, proposal, organizational decision, public measurement and later analysis.

4. Code within the source's scope. Record recurring subjects and contrasting accounts without assuming that a theme was equally prevalent across the entire country.

5. Compare across years. Check whether similar terms refer to comparable conditions, places, groups, and institutions.

6. Look for counterevidence. Record contrary examples, different participant experiences, and the limits of researcher proximity.

7. Check release rights. Verify permission, attribution, anonymity, confidentiality and organizational ownership before publishing a quotation or document.

8. Link the claim. A published statement identifies the record supporting it, how that record was read, and what the reader must not infer from it.

4 / Researcher position

Researcher position

Extended residence and professional involvement in Saudi Arabia informed the research questions and access to source material. That proximity requires careful consideration of selective access, prior assumptions, and conflicting accounts.

Firsthand observation, participant accounts, administrative reporting, and interpretation are assessed separately. Contrary evidence is retained.

For future inquiries involving accounts of exploitation, illness, or other sensitive experiences, the research protocol will also examine how collecting and interpreting those accounts affects interviewers, facilitators, and analysts. This is a prospective methodological question informed by Moghe and colleagues (Design Research Society, 2026), not evidence from the Saudi archive or a claim about safeguards used in earlier sessions.

5 / Data and quotations

Confidentiality and quotations

Private participant identities and full source records remain confidential. Direct quotations require verification and permission for public use; otherwise, findings are paraphrased without identifiers.

Author-drafted research statements are distinguished from participant testimony and measured results.

Official statistics retain their publisher, reference period, population, and measure.
