Independent research / Saudi Arabia / 2019 to 2026

Saudi Creative
Sector Research

An examination of the design industry and creative sector through original records, historical analysis, public statistics, and questions that remain unresolved.

Brandie Janow   |   Independent research   |   Began in 2019   |   Public edition, 2026

Abstract

Structural Conditions of Creative Sector Development

Saudi Arabia's expanding cultural institutions, programs, and investment provide an important account of sector development. These indicators do not independently establish the conditions under which creative professionals enter occupations, secure commissions, access technical and production resources, or sustain professional activity over time.

Initiated in 2019, this independent longitudinal research examines professional participation in Saudi Arabia's design industry and wider creative sector. It draws on original qualitative evidence, documented observations, historical research material, and public statistical sources. Two related study frameworks developed in 2025 address the supporting infrastructure of creative practice and access to financial resources.

Primary qualitative sources are maintained in a restricted research archive and analyzed without publicly identifying participants or individual research occasions. Institutional reporting and official statistics are considered separately from original evidence and interpretation. The present publication documents the research scope, questions, and historical basis while distinguishing research already conducted from additional national studies proposed in 2025.

A 2025 research brief titled Mapping the Financial Infrastructure of Saudi Arabia’s Creative Economy: Access, Barriers, and Long-Term Viability. The brief identified difficulties in understanding funding routes, eligibility, independent professional status, regulation, regional access, and the role of private finance. These were recorded as problems to examine through a proposed research study, not as conclusions of a completed national survey. [P01]

Public statistical context

Activity and access are different measures.

The official figures below offer context. They describe participation, public sector activity, or reported financing. None measures whether an independent designer could successfully access a specific opportunity or sustain a career.

81.6%People aged 15 and above reporting visits to a cultural event or activity venue in the 2024 household survey. [S01]
6,314Reported cultural event days in 2025 under the Quality of Life Program performance figures. [S03]
SAR 770m+Cumulative financial support reported by the Cultural Development Fund through the first quarter of 2026. [S04]

These figures come from different populations, programs, and time periods. They should not be merged into a single index of sector health. The sources and limits are listed in Evidence & Data.

Research question

What can the historical record actually establish?

A policy announcement records intent. A funding program records the existence of a mechanism. A participant account records experience. A later outcome can help assess what happened after the announcement, but only when the relationship between them can be established.

This publication will trace such distinctions year by year. Missing evidence remains visible. Earlier organizational affiliations and documents are subject to a separate rights review, and confidential client research is excluded from the public source collection.
