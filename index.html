Independent research / Saudi Arabia / 2019 to 2026

Saudi Creative
Sector Research

An examination of the design industry and creative sector through original records, historical analysis, public statistics, and questions that remain unresolved.

Brandie Janow   |   Independent research   |   Began in 2019   |   Public edition, 2026

Abstract

Beyond activity and announcements.

Saudi Arabia has invested in cultural institutions, programs, creative enterprises, and public participation. Documenting this activity is important. It does not, by itself, explain how people entered creative professions, obtained commissions, sustained businesses, found production resources, or remained in the sector over time.

This independent study began in 2019 and brings together an evolving archive of professional discussions, research notes, original proposals, and public material. It examines the gap between the presence of programs and the conditions of access, professional continuity, and long-term use.

The current publication makes the research questions and available sources visible without presenting incomplete analyses as settled conclusions. It treats institutional claims, participant accounts, firsthand observations, official statistics, and interpretation as separate classes of evidence.

Foundational document / 2025

Financial infrastructure was already a central question.

Research brief   |   Original draft by Brandie Janow   |   2025

In 2025, the author prepared Mapping the Financial Infrastructure of Saudi Arabia’s Creative Economy: Access, Barriers, and Long-Term Viability. The brief identified difficulties in understanding funding routes, eligibility, independent professional status, regulation, regional access, and the role of private finance. These were recorded as problems to examine through a proposed research study, not as conclusions of a completed national survey. [P01]

Public statistical context

Activity and access are different measures.

The official figures below offer context. They describe participation, public sector activity, or reported financing. None measures whether an independent designer could successfully access a specific opportunity or sustain a career.

81.6%People aged 15 and above reporting visits to a cultural event or activity venue in the 2024 household survey. [S01]
6,314Reported cultural event days in 2025 under the Quality of Life Program performance figures. [S03]
SAR 770m+Cumulative financial support reported by the Cultural Development Fund through the first quarter of 2026. [S04]

These figures come from different populations, programs, and time periods. They should not be merged into a single index of sector health. The sources and limits are listed in Evidence & Data.

Research question

What can the historical record actually establish?

A policy announcement records intent. A funding program records the existence of a mechanism. A participant account records experience. A later outcome can help assess what happened after the announcement, but only when the relationship between them can be established.

This publication will trace such distinctions year by year. Missing evidence remains visible. Earlier organizational affiliations and documents are subject to a separate rights review, and confidential client research is excluded from the public source collection.
